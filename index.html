Where Spacecraft Water Comes From

On the International Space Station, almost every drop is recycled. Water arrives from Earth in tanks, but daily operations depend on recovering moisture that the crew produces.

Humidity Condensate

Water vapor from breath, sweat, and air is condensed by cooling systems. This “crew latent” water is one of the largest recoverable streams and is collected continuously.

Urine & Flush Water

Urine is pretreated with chemicals, then processed. Combined with flush water, this stream is distilled to recover most of its water content before further purification.

Hygiene & Other

Water from hand washing, oral hygiene, and limited washing is captured. Sabatier product water (from CO₂ reduction) also feeds the system when available.

Earth Resupply

Clean water is still launched in contingency water containers and tanks to top up reserves, replace losses, and provide a reliable backup supply.

Collection, Filtration & Purification

The Water Recovery System (WRS) has two main assemblies that turn wastewater into potable water that meets or exceeds Earth drinking-water standards.

Urine Processor Assembly (UPA)

Pretreated urine is distilled in a rotating centrifuge under vacuum. Water evaporates at lower temperature and is condensed, leaving a concentrated brine. Typical recovery from urine is 70–87% depending on pretreatment.

Brine Processor Assembly (BPA)

The remaining brine is further processed so that warm air evaporates additional water through a special membrane. This step helps push overall system recovery above 98%.

Water Processor Assembly (WPA)

Distillate, condensate, and other streams enter the WPA:

  • Multifiltration beds remove particulates, organics, and ions
  • Catalytic reactor oxidizes remaining organic compounds with heat and oxygen
  • Ion-exchange and polishing steps finish the water
  • Conductivity and other sensors check quality before the water is released to storage

The final product is pure enough for drinking, food preparation, and oxygen generation.

Storage, Distribution & Reuse

Clean water is stored in tanks and bags, then distributed through the potable bus to the crew and to systems that need it.

Storage

Product water is held in dedicated tanks and flexible Contingency Water Containers. Reserves are maintained so the habitat can operate for weeks if processing stops.

Distribution

Pumps and lines deliver water to dispensers for drinking and food, to the toilet for flush, and to the Oxygen Generation Assembly for electrolysis.

Closed Loop

With the Brine Processor operating, the ISS has demonstrated total water recovery of 98–99%. Only a small percentage is lost to leaks, airlock operations, or residual brine.

Drinking Water & Hygiene

Drinking & Food

Each crew member needs roughly 2 liters of water per day for drinking and food rehydration. Dispensers provide hot and cold water. The recycled product is cleaner than most municipal tap water on Earth.

Hygiene

Limited water is used for oral hygiene, hand washing, and body cleaning. Special low-water techniques and wipes help keep total use low while maintaining cleanliness.

System Uses

Clean water is also fed to the Oxygen Generation Assembly for electrolysis and used as flush water in the toilet. Every use is designed so the water can be recovered again.

Equipment Maintenance, Contamination & Leaks

Routine Maintenance

Astronauts replace multifiltration beds, change filters, clean sensors, and swap Orbital Replacement Units. Many components are designed for on-orbit replacement so the system stays online.

Contamination Control

Pretreatment chemicals, filters, and the catalytic reactor prevent microbial growth and remove organics. Continuous conductivity and other sensors detect problems before water is released to the crew.

Leak Detection

Pressure and flow sensors watch for leaks in lines and tanks. Small leaks are managed with seals and isolation valves. Larger events trigger contingency procedures and use of stored reserves.

Emergency Reserves & Contingencies

Multiple independent supplies ensure water is always available even if primary processors fail.

Stored Potable Water

Hundreds to thousands of liters of clean water are kept in tanks and bags. This reserve protects against processor downtime lasting days to weeks.

Resupply Capability

Cargo vehicles can deliver additional Contingency Water Containers if needed. Planning accounts for possible delays in resupply.

Isolation & Bypass

Valves allow crews to isolate a failed unit and continue using stored water or a backup processor while repairs are made.

Current Spacecraft vs Future Concepts

Current (ISS & Near-Term)

  • UPA distillation + BPA brine processing
  • WPA multifiltration and catalytic oxidation
  • 98–99% total water recovery demonstrated
  • Regular filter and ORU replacement by crew
  • Earth resupply of water and spare parts still required
  • Conductivity and sensor-based quality checks

Future Concepts

  • Higher reliability and longer-life components
  • Reduced crew maintenance time
  • Membrane and reverse-osmosis improvements
  • In-situ resource utilization (lunar or Martian ice)
  • More closed-loop systems for deep-space transit
  • Still based on physical and chemical processes — no dependence on growing crops

Future habitats will aim for even higher recovery and lower resupply mass while keeping robust emergency reserves and clear water-quality monitoring.
